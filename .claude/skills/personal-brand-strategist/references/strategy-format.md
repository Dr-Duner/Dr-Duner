# Output Format

Two deliverables: a markdown working document and an HTML artifact for
reading and sharing. Same content, different purposes — the markdown gets
edited and version-controlled, the artifact gets read and sent to people.

## Document structure

Use these sections in this order. Skip a section only when the interview
genuinely produced nothing for it, and say so rather than padding.

```markdown
# Personal Brand Strategy — [Name]
*Drafted [date]*

## The claim
[One sentence. The thing to be known for.]

## Who this is for
[The specific audience. Concrete enough to picture one person.
Include who it deliberately excludes.]

## The wedge
[Why this person, on this topic, credibly. The intersection of what they
uniquely know, what the audience needs, and what nobody else is saying.]

## Point of view
[The arguable thesis, with the reasoning attached. 2–3 sentences.]

## Proof
[Credentials, artifacts, results, scars — mapped to the claims they support.
Note the gaps honestly.]

## The landscape
[Who currently owns this audience's attention, what they miss, where the
opening is.]

## Channels
[Primary and secondary, with the reasoning. Why these and not the others.]

## Formats
[3–4 repeatable formats, each with one worked example built from the
person's real material.]

## Cadence
[Specific, sustainable, with the batching rhythm.]

## 90 days
[Week-by-week, front-loaded, starting with something doable today.]

## Measuring it
[Leading vs lagging indicators, and what to ignore early.]

## Open questions
[What's still unresolved and what would resolve it.]
```

## Writing standards

**Write in their voice, not brand voice.** Pull phrasing directly from the
interview. If they said "the lab budget was forty dollars, so we built it out
of hardware-store parts," that sentence is better than anything composed to
replace it, and it belongs in the document as-is.

**Every claim gets a reason.** A strategy document that asserts without
explaining can't be argued with, which means it can't be improved. Say why
LinkedIn over X. Say why twice a week and not daily. The reasoning is what
lets them adapt it when circumstances change — and they will.

**Be concrete enough to act on.** "Post consistently about your expertise" is
not actionable. "Every Tuesday, post one case from the week — what came in,
what you did, what surprised you" is.

**Keep it short enough to reread.** Two to four pages. A twenty-page strategy
gets read once and never opened again, which makes it worthless regardless of
quality.

**Flag the uncertain.** Where a recommendation rests on an assumption, name
it. "This assumes you're willing to be identified as a practising clinician
publicly — if not, the whole channel plan changes." That's more useful than
false confidence, and it tells them exactly which decision to revisit.

## The artifact

Build from `assets/strategy-template.html`. Replace the placeholder content,
keep the structure and styling.

Follow the Artifact tool's requirements — the design skill (`artifact-design`)
should be loaded before writing it, and the page must be self-contained and
theme-aware. The template already handles the theme tokens; don't strip them.

Publish it privately and hand over the link. Mention it's private by default
and theirs to share.

## After delivering

End with **one clear next action** — usually the Week 1 bio rewrite. Offer to
do that immediately; it takes a few minutes and converts a document into
momentum.

Then ask whether the positioning felt right. The first draft is a hypothesis,
and the person's reaction to reading it is data the interview couldn't
produce. Expect a round of revision, and treat a strong negative reaction to
one option as useful rather than as failure — it usually means the interview
under-weighted something they care about.
