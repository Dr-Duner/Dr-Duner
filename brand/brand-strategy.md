# Personal Brand Strategy
*Drafted 2026-08-22 · pseudonymous · scope: social + search*

**Channel name: Blame the Dentist** (pending handle availability check on
YouTube + Reddit). Chosen over the tooth-pun family, which is saturated
— "Tooth Be Told" alone has four active properties — and which signals
dental-insider content to a parent audience. Carries the "dentist"
keyword, encodes the actual conflict, and inverts into the point as the
audience grows.

## The claim

> Twenty years in a pediatric dental chair. Your kid isn't bad at the
> dentist — and most of what went wrong was decided before you walked in.

Substance from "Before" (the preparation is the fix), hook from "After"
(the parent arrives already believing their child is the problem).

## Who this is for

Parents of **2–6 year olds** — the window where autonomy has arrived and
language hasn't caught up — who have a dental appointment coming, or who
have just had one go badly and think their child is the problem.

**Deliberately excludes:** other dentists, dental students, practice
owners, parents of teenagers, and anyone looking for clinical education.
Not a professional audience. The audience is the dad in the operatory.

## The wedge

Sixteen encounters a day, since 2006, at the exact moment a child's
autonomy collides with an adult's expectation of compliance. That's not
knowledge from reading — it's a sample size no parenting writer has and
no practice blog is allowed to use.

The landscape check confirms the lane is open for a structural reason:
practice-branded content cannot indict the parents it is trying to
convert. A pseudonymous clinician can. **The anonymity is the enabler,
not the constraint.**

## Point of view

> "The child is the variable both adults have no control over. The
> dentist understands the autonomy the dad does not."

> "Parents think their kids are just little adults sometimes. They are
> not and do not operate that way in a dental chair."

A four-year-old pulling at the nasal hood is **information, not
defiance**. The parent's anger in that room isn't really about the
child's behavior — it's about discovering that control they assumed they
had doesn't exist. The job is preparation, not compliance.

This is arguable, which is the point. Plenty of parents and some
clinicians believe a child should comply because the adult said so.

## Tone

**Ironic frame, sincere content.** The name carries the joke so the
videos don't have to press it. Each piece is genuinely useful; the
wrapper is dry.

**The rule: the joke is on the dentist or the situation, never on the
viewer.** A parent arriving after a bad visit often already suspects
they failed. Irony aimed at them produces defensiveness and a closed
tab; shame does not change booking behavior. Irony aimed at the dentist
lets them laugh along, and the conclusion lands on its own without ever
being pointed at them. Same information, opposite outcome.

Give them a shared enemy that isn't each other: the 5pm slot, the myth
that children are small adults, the cheerful advice that omits all of
this.

| | Wrong | Right |
|---|---|---|
| The 5pm slot | "You booked a 5pm filling for a four-year-old. What did you think would happen?" | "It's my fault, obviously. I'm the one who scheduled a four-year-old for a filling at the exact hour his nervous system clocks out. Oh — I didn't? Huh." |
| The helicopter | "Stop hovering over me while I work." | "I've had a parent lean in close enough that I was functionally doing dentistry on two people. I cannot think of another job where this happens. Pilots don't have this." |

The setup implicates the dentist; the reveal does the work. This is the
register no practice-branded channel can copy, because a named practice
cannot be wry about its own customers.

## Proof

The pseudonym means credentials can't be verified, so proof has to be
**demonstrated, not claimed** — being specifically right in ways only
someone with the reps could be.

| Type | What | Supports |
|---|---|---|
| Scar | 20 years, every day, since 2006 — including the days he wanted to walk out and didn't | Durability; that this isn't theory |
| Result | Distraction technique works on **75%+** of kids | The other 25% is the known limit of a method, not operator failure |
| Artifact | The four-parent taxonomy — controller, helicopter, oblivious, override | Pattern recognition at scale |
| Scar | Calling an appointment is the correct clinical call *and* the one that gets you reported to management | Stakes; why nobody else says this |

**Gap:** no public artifacts yet. First 90 days exist to create them.

## The landscape

Searched 2026-08-22. Page one for both the "before" and "after" queries
is entirely practice-marketing blogs plus institutional sources (ADA,
Colgate, Stanford, WebMD, PBS). Uniform advice: talk positively, read a
book, practice at home, bring a toy, stay calm, book mornings.

- **"Book mornings" is already standard advice.** The novelty is not the
  fix — it's that parents are told and override it anyway.
- **Nothing addresses failure honestly.** All reassurance, no autopsy.
- **Nothing addresses parent behavior.** Structurally impossible for a
  named practice. This is the lane.

## Channels

**Primary — faceless YouTube.** Chosen for *search*, not social. YouTube
is the second-largest search engine and this is a search-shaped topic:
parents type these questions at 11pm the night before, or the night
after. Content gets found for years. Works with no face, no name, no
employer. Production pipeline already exists from Theta — animated
characters, scripted VO, storyboards — so cadence is limited by writing,
not by learning video.

**Secondary — Reddit** (r/Parenting, r/toddlers, r/Mommit). Where these
parents already ask, and pseudonymity is the norm rather than a
handicap. Also functions as topic research: their questions are the
content calendar.

**Later, not now:** newsletter (owned surface, no algorithm), podcast
guesting (voice-only, no face), a paid preparation guide.

**Ruled out:** LinkedIn — identity-bound, incompatible with the pseudonym.

## Formats

**1. The autopsy** — one appointment that went wrong, decoded. What the
parent saw vs. what was actually happening.
*Example:* The 5pm four-year-old. Nitrous goes on, kid pulls at the
nasal hood, dad tells him to stop moving, then looks at the dentist to
continue — because dad said so, not because the patient agreed.

**2. The thing nobody tells you** — one specific pre-visit decision that
changes the outcome.
*Example:* Book the 8am. A four-year-old at 5pm has been running down
since three o'clock. You control the appointment time. It is the single
biggest variable you own, and you are throwing it away.

**3. Which parent are you?** — the taxonomy, one type per piece.
*Example:* The helicopter. "I cannot think of another profession where
someone stands over you and watches every movement of your hands." Why
it makes the visit worse, and what to do with your body instead.

**4. Translation** — a child behavior, read correctly.
*Example:* Pulling at the mask isn't defiance. It's a sensory report
from someone without the vocabulary to file it.

## Cadence

**Floor: one piece per week.** Planned against a bad week, not a good
one. Stated availability is 32 hrs/week; the plan is sized to roughly 10
so that a rough month reads as normal rather than as failure. Anything
above the floor is upside — bank it, don't spend it raising the floor.

Batch on a single writing day: script two or three at a time. Finding
time on the day is what kills cadence.

## Measuring it

Year-one goal is **validation** — "seeing if this is even possible" — not
subscribers. So:

**Leading (weeks)** — did the piece ship; do comments show parents
recognizing themselves; do the same questions recur; does any piece get
found via search rather than feed.

**Lagging (months)** — watch time on the autopsy format, subscriber
count, saves/shares.

**Ignore early:** follower count. Five parents saying "I booked the
morning slot because of this" beats five hundred passive views.

**Kill criterion:** if at week 12 nothing has been found via search and
no comment shows a parent changing a decision, the format is wrong —
not the person, and not the thesis.
